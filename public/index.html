🪵 Growth Rings note
A good decision archive helps you see how the organization grew. The goal is not to prove you were right — it is to record what information was available, what tradeoff you chose, and when the choice should be revisited.

One-way door

Hard to reverse. Examples: hiring, firing, cap table changes, customer commitments, legal commitments, expensive purchases.

Two-way door

Easy to reverse. Examples: a temporary process, experiment, landing page, outreach script, or default meeting cadence.

Review rule

For one-way doors, add extra context and review monthly. For two-way doors, move fast and revisit if the decision creates repeated friction.

Field guide

Use this as the team norm for making the archive useful rather than bureaucratic.

1. Log the tradeoff

Future people need to know what was sacrificed, not just what was chosen.

  • Speed vs. certainty
  • Cost vs. quality
  • Customer value vs. scientific elegance

2. Save rejected options

This prevents the team from re-running the same conversation later.

  • What did we not do?
  • Why was it rejected?
  • What would make it worth revisiting?

3. Review patterns

The efficiency gain comes from noticing how you decide over time.

  • Are we over-indexing on speed?
  • Are decisions getting stuck?
  • Which categories create rework?
